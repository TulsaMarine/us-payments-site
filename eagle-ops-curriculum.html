AI Recon: How AI Can Help You Win Your Next Mission

Presented by: Chuck Conner — Marine Veteran, Construction Business Owner, AI Practitioner Host Organization: Eagle OPS (Owasso, OK) — eagleops.org Duration: 90 minutes (with 10-minute break) Setting: Conference room with projector Audience: Veterans with zero technical background

Learning Objectives

By the end of this class, participants will be able to:

  1. Explain what AI is in plain English — and what it isn't
  2. Use a free AI tool (ChatGPT, Gemini, or Claude) to get a useful answer to a real question
  3. Identify 3–5 ways AI can help them personally — job hunting, benefits, small business, daily life
  4. Recognize AI's limitations — hallucinations, privacy risks, and when to trust (or not trust) the output

Materials Needed

  • Presenter: Laptop connected to projector, internet connection, browser with ChatGPT/Claude open
  • Participants: Smartphone or laptop (optional but encouraged)
  • Handouts: Printed takeaway sheet (see below) — or distribute digitally via QR code

Time Activity

2:00–5:00 What is AI? Break it down: AI is pattern recognition + language at scale. It's not Skynet, not sentient, not magic. It's a tool — like going from a hand saw to a power saw. Military analogy: "AI is like having a really smart private who never sleeps, never complains, and always has an answer — but you still have to check their work."

Key talking points:

  • You talk to it like a person. That's it. That's the interface.

Time Demo

The Catch — What AI Can't Do (10 min)

Time Topic
70:00–73:00 Hallucinations. AI sometimes makes things up — confidently. It'll cite fake court cases, invent statistics, give wrong phone numbers. ALWAYS verify important info. "Trust but verify" — Reagan had it right.
73:00–76:00 Privacy & Security. DO NOT put your SSN, passwords, medical records, or financial account numbers into free AI tools. Treat it like talking to a stranger in a coffee shop — helpful, but not your doctor or lawyer.
76:00–78:00 Not a replacement for human connection. AI can draft a letter, but it can't sit across from you at a Rally Point. That's what Eagle OPS is for.
78:00–80:00 You still drive. AI is a tool, not a crutch. You make the decisions. It handles the busywork. Think of it as your staff — you're still the CO.

Quick-Start Prompts to Try

Copy these exactly — or modify for your situation:

  1. Resume: "Write a resume for a veteran with [X years] of military experience transitioning to [civilian job]. Translate military skills into civilian language."
  2. Benefits: "Explain VA disability compensation in plain English. What's the process to file a claim?"
  3. Business: "I want to start a [type] business in [city, state]. Give me a simple plan with startup costs and first steps."
  4. Job Prep: "Give me 10 common interview questions for [job title] and help me prepare strong answers."
  5. Daily Life: "Create a weekly meal plan for [number] people on a $[amount] budget with a shopping list."
  6. Letter Writing: "Help me write a professional letter to [recipient] about [topic]."

Pro Tips

  • Be specific. "Help me write a resume" is okay. "Help me write a resume for a project management role, I was an E-6 with 8 years in logistics" is better.
  • Iterate. If the first answer isn't perfect, say "Make it shorter" or "More professional tone" or "Add more detail about X."
  • Ask it to explain. "Explain this like I'm 10" works great for complex topics.
  • Don't trust blindly. Verify facts, especially numbers, dates, legal info, and medical advice.
